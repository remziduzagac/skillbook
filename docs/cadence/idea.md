## What it is for

cadence is the rhythm of your own working days: meetings, tasks, and the reviews that close a day or a week. Essentials makes an agent do any single piece of work well. cadence helps with the work that comes back again and again, so nothing that matters slips between days.

What you learn and want to keep, such as notes and sources, belongs to a separate set for knowledge. cadence hands notes to it rather than keeping them itself.

## Its skills

- **meetings:** gets the decisions and actions out of a meeting, each action with its owner and due date, and the words it rests on.
- **tasks:** your own tasks: what is for today, what waits on someone, what is blocked, and what each belongs to.
- **reflect:** daily, weekly and monthly reviews: what was done, what moved, what is next.
- **close-out:** wraps up a piece of work: keeps what matters, cleans up the rest, and hands what is worth keeping to the right place.
- **triage:** turns incoming requests or issues into clear briefs an agent can act on.
- **focus:** sets up a focused work session and keeps it on its goal.
- **people:** one-to-ones, onboarding and feedback.

## How it fits

- **Built on essentials:** cadence uses essentials' rules for understanding, acting and answering, and its carryover for work that continues in a later session.
- **Apart from design work:** meetings and triage are daily work, not design. A meeting's decisions about a project still go to that project's design record.
- **Plain files:** everything cadence keeps is plain text you can read and move.

## Open questions

- Where cadence keeps its files: beside a project, in a folder of your own, or both.
- Whether cadence and the knowledge set share one folder.
- How reviews and close-out hand lessons to essentials' learning from a session.
- Which of focus and people are worth a skill of their own.
