# understand-clarify-shallow

Gets the full picture of a level before going further: the smallest round of questions that sets its direction, with a recommended answer where there is a basis, then "going with" what was answered and chosen.

## Rules

1. **The whole level first:** the level is the request's top level when clarify starts, or a level inside a part when deep reaches one. See every part of it before any part goes deeper, because which part needs depth shows only once the whole is seen. Ask the smallest round that sets its direction, and later questions when their answers become needed, because a long round on routine work costs the user more than it settles.
2. **One round,** put as `respond-ask` says, so the user answers it at once and sees the level whole.
3. **"Going with: …" before work starts:** sum up the decisions the work rests on, the assumptions you took and what is still open, so the user sees what the work rests on. Don't say back an answer the user just gave unless you had to read something into it. Leave out minor choices, by the `understand` skill's rule on who decides, because a line that repeats everything hides what matters.

## Advice

Ask first the questions that others depend on. There is no fixed number of questions.

## Instructions

1. **Look across the whole level,** first from what can be read. Ask about what you could not settle from what is known and is the user's by clarify's first rule, such as how long a newsletter runs. A choice only a likely value could settle, and yours to make, such as how many rows a new list view shows per page: take the likely value and state it in "going with", never quietly.
2. **Leave out** what clarify's rules say not to ask. If no choice is left for the user, ask nothing: say "going with" and go on.
3. **Ask in one round,** each question with its recommended answer and a short reason, as `respond-ask` says.
4. **End the turn** with the round, as `respond-ask` says, and take the answers when the user replies.
5. **Hand over:** a part may have answers that depend on each other and would change the work, or you may be unsure whether they do. That ends shallow for that part. Offer deep on it, as the last instruction says, and ask no deep question before a yes. Deep can take many rounds, and the user may not see why more questions come, or may prefer to go on with your guesses.
   - After a yes, the part goes back to clarify, which loads deep.
   - If deep called shallow, the user has said yes already. Make no offer; the part goes back to deep.

You are done when every question in the round is answered, taken with its recommended answer, parked, or in a questionnaire, or when its part has been offered to deep and the user has said yes or no.

## The last instruction

Before work starts, say "going with: …" for the rest, as rule 3 says: the decisions and assumptions the work rests on, and what was parked or is in a questionnaire.

In the same response, offer deep for each part handed over:

- Name the part, and say why it needs more questions: its answers depend on each other and would change the work.
- Ask, as `respond-ask` says, whether to question the user on it. Recommend yes, with a short reason. "Go ahead" accepts it.
- If the user declines, treat that part's open questions as `respond-ask` treats questions left open when the user stops: one with a recommended answer is taken with it, one without is parked, and nothing only the user knows is supplied. Say both in "going with" before any work on the part starts.

Hand it back to clarify. What comes next is the work, or deep on a part after a yes.
