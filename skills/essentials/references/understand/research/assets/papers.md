# Papers as sources

Use this file for research papers and literature overviews. Record the author's actual claim, scope, method and evidence. Do not promote an illustrative example into a general result.

Trace summaries to the paper where available. Check whether apparent independent evidence repeats one study. Distinguish correlation from a causal result, a single case from a pattern, and a claim supported by the method from one it does not establish.

For a wide literature search, vary terms and search older and newer work. Watch for term drift and recurring authors or repeat hits. Citation counts per year can help you find papers. They are not proof that a paper is right. Never invent one. Keep inclusion criteria and screened-out work visible under survey's rules.

If full text or needed evidence is inaccessible, return the abstract or summary only with its actual provenance and limits. Run a literature review with the `understand-research-survey` guide. Do not invent a confidence score.
