# Papers as sources

Use this guide for research papers and literature overviews. Record the author's actual claim, scope, method and evidence. Do not promote an illustrative example into a general result.

Trace summaries to the paper where available. Check whether apparent independent evidence repeats one study. Distinguish correlation from a causal result, a single case from a pattern, and a claim supported by the method from one it does not establish.

For a wide literature search, vary terms and search older and newer work. Watch for term drift and recurring authors or repeat hits. Citation counts per year may guide discovery when known; they are not proof of truth or a required invented number. Keep inclusion criteria and screened-out work visible under survey's rules.

If full text or needed evidence is inaccessible, return the abstract or summary only with its actual provenance and limits. A literature review uses the existing survey method; add no new method or false confidence score.
