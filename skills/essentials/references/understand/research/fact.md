# understand-research-fact

## Purpose

Answers one sourced question or gives a bounded verdict on one claim.

## When to use

Use when an investigation needs one sourced answer or a verdict on one claim that one local source does not answer by itself. For example, a claim that something cannot be done needs evidence for that limit.

## When not

- Existing alternatives: use the `understand-research-survey` guide to find and compare candidates.
- Causes: use the `understand-research-analyze` guide to test why something happens.
- One local source that answers the question by itself: read it directly. No sourced investigation is needed. A claim that needs several sources still needs this guide, even when they are local.
- User intent: use the `understand-clarify` guide. A source cannot decide what the user wants.

## Gives back

The answer or claim verdict, supporting and opposing evidence, limits and missing checks.

## Rules

1. **Search against a claim as seriously as for it.** Searching only for support rewards confirmation. Keep the actual evidence found on each side, including no evidence found.
2. **Judge by relevant evidence strength rather than the number of findings.** One current applicable primary statement can outweigh many copies. Stale authority or evidence for another case does not override the actual case.
3. **Keep verdicts distinct from missing checks.** Use one of five verdicts:
   - supported: the relevant evidence supports the claim;
   - mixed: relevant evidence both supports and challenges it;
   - unsupported: you finished the check and found no adequate support;
   - contradicted: relevant evidence that applies to the case shows the claim is false, such as a newer rule that replaces the one the claim rests on;
   - unknown: access, coverage or evidence kept you from a reliable verdict.

   When applicable evidence outweighs the rest by rule 2, the verdict is supported or contradicted, not mixed. Stale or inapplicable evidence on the other side does not make it mixed.

   Only contradicted shows the claim false. A check that found no support is not evidence against the claim, so unsupported and unknown never do. Give the evidence, its limits and what would change the verdict.

## Advice

- For a claim, name the hypothesis and search opposing terms, counterexamples, negations, comparisons, later dates and the other side's view.
- Spend about a third of the searches for a claim on evidence against it. That is enough to test the claim, not just confirm it. It is advice about the search, not a quota or a vote over results.
- A claim that something cannot be done needs an observed error, an applicable documented statement or a permitted probe. Without one, it is only said.
- Keep the answer as short as the question permits while preserving evidence and limitations.

## Instructions

Answer one sourced question or judge one claim within the evidence’s actual scope. A completed search without support differs from a check that access or coverage prevented. **Reuse the question, search relevant evidence both ways for a claim, then return the bounded answer or verdict.**

1. **Identify or reuse the question through rule 1 of the `understand-research` guide.** Evidence for a neighboring question can mislead the caller just as much as a wrong fact.
   - Keep a question that is already clear, and the current frame. Do not frame it again or ask the user to choose again.
2. **Search relevant sources using the `understand-research` guide and rule 1 here.** Looking only for support can turn the first plausible account into a false certainty.
   - Start with the nearest relevant source and the file for its kind of source. Check version, platform and date.
   - For a claim, search opposing terms, counterexamples, negations and later evidence as seriously as support. About a third against is guidance, not a quota.
   - A claim of impossibility needs an observed error, applicable documentation or a permitted probe. Otherwise keep it only said.
3. **Rank findings by how they were obtained, through rule 3 of the `understand-research` guide.** Repeated copies or an exact quote can look strong without supporting the actual claim.
   - Keep supporting, contrary and missing evidence visible. Use relevant strength rather than source count, by rule 2 here.
   - Mark memory, inference and unread source leads honestly. Keep the cases the evidence applies to beside its strength.
4. **Return the answer or a bounded verdict under rule 3.** Conflating unknown with unsupported can make the caller reject a claim that was never adequately checked.
   - For a claim, choose supported, mixed, unsupported, contradicted or unknown from the actual check. Only contradicted shows the claim false; unsupported and unknown never do.
   - Give evidence for and against, checked scope, limits, missing checks and what could change the verdict. Return the factual result to naming or comparison without taking over their choice.
5. **Check the answer and “Gives back” before returning it.** Always do this last. The caller needs the evidence and the limit together to use the result safely.
   - Check claims were searched both ways and evidence fits the actual version and scope. Use the parent's finding fields and result shape.
   - Correct an overstated verdict or a missing check before returning. Then follow the final step of the `understand-research` guide, and return to the guide that asked without starting it again.
