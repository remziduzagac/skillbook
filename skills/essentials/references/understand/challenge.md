# understand-challenge

## Purpose

Attacks an idea, plan or decision before it is relied on and returns meaningful weaknesses and a verdict.

## When to use

Use before relying on an idea, plan or decision, or when asked to find its weak points. For example, an unchecked assumption may break a plan. The `respond-ask` guide also calls this guide before a consequential recommendation or decision, by rule 6.

## When not

- Several options to compare: use the `understand-weigh` guide to judge them on the same terms.
- A design review: use the `devise-design-review` guide. It calls this guide for its attack on the design's ideas.
- Finished work about to land: use a skill in your harness's list for reviewing that kind of work, if one fits. Otherwise use your judgment under the main skill's step 5. This guide attacks an idea before it is relied on.
- A claim that work is done: use the `act-verify` guide for evidence.
- An unclear idea: use the `understand-clarify` guide or the `understand-frame` guide before trying to find its weak points.
- A doubt settled by facts: use sources or a test. An objection alone is not evidence.
- A case for a position: use the `understand-argue` guide to build the strongest honest argument.

## Gives back

Weak points that matter to the goal, including costs or risks even when success stays possible, each with its seriousness and remedy, then an honest verdict. When the user asked for the challenge, show this default shape:

```text
Idea:         its strongest fair version
Measured by:  goal, constraints and a good answer
Assumptions:  each one, checked or unchecked
Weaknesses:   each starting with its seriousness, then its evidence and remedy; most serious first
Verdict:      holds, holds with changes (which), or does not hold (why)
```

When you challenge an idea without being asked, such as your own plan before you recommend it, show only the verdict and the serious weaknesses, each with its reason, unless the user asks for more. The skill that saves records gets the challenge, the weaknesses considered, how each was met, fixed or accepted as a risk, and those still open.

## Rules

1. **Measure against the frame.** Use the `understand-frame` guide's goal and constraints. Report weaknesses that block the goal or create material cost or risk in scope, even if success remains feasible. Exclude unrelated preferences. Otherwise any flaw counts and the list never ends.
2. **Name assumptions as checked or unchecked.** List each assumption the idea rests on in the answer, with its mark. Unchecked assumptions are where most ideas break.
3. **Give each weakness its seriousness, evidence and remedy.** Start each weakness with its seriousness: stops it, costs, or minor. Without all three, a finding cannot be acted on.
4. **Give an honest verdict.** Use holds, holds with changes, or does not hold. Say nothing found plainly. Soften nothing to please and invent nothing to look thorough. A flattering challenge is worse than none.
5. **Change nothing.** The main skill's step 1 rules hold. The caller develops or revises the idea. The user decides.
6. **When the `respond-ask` guide calls you, challenge only a decision that is hard to undo or shapes much of the work.** That guide's test decides which. Routine questions need no challenge just because they are in a plan or design.
   - Scale your check with the `act-stakes` guide: at low stakes, make no challenge; at medium stakes, think through the strongest one or two objections; at high stakes, make a full challenge. Show medium-stakes objections when they hold and change the recommendation or add a caveat. Put the verdict beside the recommendation as the `respond-ask` guide requires.
7. **Reuse an earlier challenge only while the idea, goal, constraints and evidence are unchanged.** A changed input can make its findings wrong. Unchanged inputs need no second pass, whichever guide asks, such as the `respond-ask`, `understand-weigh` or `understand-argue` guide.

## Advice

- Imagine failure a year from now and explain why.
- Treat an unsupported claim as an assumption. Seek evidence rather than treating demand or preference as known.

## Instructions

Test the strongest fair version of an idea against its goal and constraints. Look for weaknesses that block the goal or create material cost or risk, rather than collecting unrelated flaws. **Frame the idea fairly, examine its assumptions, then give an honest verdict without changing it.**

1. **Give the idea at its strongest using the author's case and the `understand-frame` guide.** Attacking a weaker version leaves the real idea untested and gives its owner false reassurance.
   - Reuse the goal and constraints. If the idea is unclear, settle that first through framing or clarification.
2. **Examine assumptions and meaningful weaknesses under rules 1–3, 6 and 7.** Unchecked assumptions can break the work after the caller has committed to it.
   - Mark assumptions checked or unchecked. Look for missing evidence, terms, constraints and dependencies.
   - Try relevant failure scenarios: tenfold size, a year later, the main assumption false, a changed dependency, or the person who knows it gone. Keep findings tied to the goal, cost or risk.
   - Give each weakness evidence, a remedy and seriousness: stops it, costs or minor. Prefer a few strong weaknesses. Group common causes and put the most serious first.
   - Use the `understand-research` guide or a permitted test for factual doubts. At high stakes, have a fresh agent make the objections, by the `act-verify` guide's rule 2. Your own second look is not independent.
   - When the `respond-ask` guide calls, scale the check by rule 6. Reuse an earlier challenge only while rule 7 allows it.
3. **Return an honest verdict under rules 4 and 5.** Invented objections waste the owner's effort; softened ones hide a reason to change course.
   - Give holds, holds with changes, or does not hold, with reasons. Say plainly when nothing meaningful was found.
   - Return the weaknesses and how each was met, fixed or accepted as a risk, in the form "Gives back" sets. Change nothing here.
4. **Check the challenge and “Gives back” before returning it.** Always do this last. A verdict without support or remedies cannot guide the caller's decision.
   - Check the fair idea, frame, assumption labels, evidence, seriousness and remedies. Check that reused results are current by rule 7, and that a high-stakes check came from a fresh agent.
   - Fix weak or unsupported findings before returning under the main skill's step 1 rules. Keep lasting records and revisions with their owner.
