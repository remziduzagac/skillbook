# understand-challenge

## Purpose

Attacks an idea, plan or decision before it is relied on and returns meaningful weaknesses and a verdict.

## When to use

Use before relying on an idea, plan or decision, or when asked to find its weak points. For example, an unchecked assumption may break a plan. When the `respond-ask` guide calls this guide, follow its test for a decision that is hard to undo or shapes much of the work, and the levels in rule 6 below.

## When not

- Several options to compare: use the `understand-weigh` guide to judge them on the same terms.
- Finished work or a design review: use the skill that reviews that kind of work. This guide attacks an idea before it is relied on.
- A claim that work is done: use the `act-verify` guide for evidence.
- An unclear idea: use the `understand-clarify` guide or the `understand-frame` guide before trying to find its weak points.
- A doubt settled by facts: use sources or a test. An objection alone is not evidence.
- A case for a position: use the `understand-argue` guide to build the strongest honest argument.

## Gives back

Weak points that matter to the goal, including costs or risks even when success remains possible. Give each one’s seriousness and remedy, then an honest verdict.

## Rules

1. **Measure against the frame.** Use the `understand-frame` guide's goal and constraints. Report weaknesses that block the goal or create material cost or risk in scope, even if success remains feasible. Exclude unrelated preferences. Otherwise any flaw counts and the list never ends.
2. **Name assumptions as checked or unchecked.** Unchecked assumptions are where most ideas break.
3. **Give each weakness its seriousness, evidence and remedy.** Rank it as stops it, costs, or minor. Without these, a finding cannot be acted on.
4. **Give an honest verdict.** Use holds, holds with changes, or does not hold. Say nothing found plainly. Soften nothing to please and invent nothing to look thorough. A flattering challenge is worse than none.
5. **Change nothing.** The `understand` guide's rules hold. The caller develops or revises the idea. The user decides.
6. **Use the `respond-ask` guide's test for a decision that is hard to undo or shapes much of the work when it calls you.** Routine questions need no challenge merely because they are in a plan or design.
   - Scale your check with the `act-stakes` guide: at low stakes, make no challenge; at medium stakes, think through the strongest one or two objections; at high stakes, make a full challenge. Show medium-stakes objections when they hold and change the recommendation or add a caveat. Put the verdict beside the recommendation as the `respond-ask` guide requires.
   - Reuse an earlier challenge only while the idea, goal, constraints and evidence stay unchanged. Accepting the same recommendation needs no second pass. Changed inputs can invalidate findings.

## Advice

- Imagine failure a year from now and explain why.
- Try relevant scenarios: tenfold size, a year later, a false main assumption, a changed dependency or the knowledgeable person gone.
- Look for missing evidence, undefined terms, unstated constraints and dependencies.
- Treat an unsupported claim as an assumption. Seek evidence rather than treating demand or preference as known.
- For who supplies independent objections, follow the `act-verify` guide's rule 2 and use the `coordinate-delegate` guide for the required worker. This guide owns that check. The `understand-argue` guide and the `understand-weigh` guide may reuse it while inputs remain unchanged.
- Prefer a few strong weaknesses. Put the most serious first and group common causes.

## Instructions

Test the strongest fair version of an idea against its goal and constraints. Look for weaknesses that block the goal or create material cost or risk, rather than collecting unrelated flaws. **Frame the idea fairly, examine its assumptions, then give an honest verdict without changing it.**

To settle a factual doubt found during the challenge, use the `understand-research` guide or a permitted test. Return the result here. Keep this guide's ownership of the independent check.

Return this short default shape:

```text
Idea:         its strongest fair version
Measured by:  goal, constraints and a good answer
Assumptions:  checked or unchecked
Weaknesses:   seriousness, evidence and remedy; grouped, most serious first
Verdict:      holds, holds with changes (which), or does not hold (why)
```

Keep no lasting record yourself. Return the challenge, the weaknesses you considered, how each was met, fixed or accepted as a risk with its reason, and open weaknesses for the skill that saves records. For your own idea analyzed quietly, show the verdict and serious weaknesses with reasons unless the user asks for more.

1. **Give the idea at its strongest using the author's case and the `understand-frame` guide.** Attacking a weaker version leaves the real idea untested and gives its owner false reassurance.
   - Reuse the goal and constraints. If the idea is unclear, settle that first through framing or clarification.
2. **Examine assumptions and meaningful weaknesses under rules 1–3 and 6.** Unchecked assumptions can break the work after the caller has committed to it.
   - Mark assumptions checked or unchecked. Look for missing evidence, terms, constraints and dependencies.
   - Try relevant failure scenarios, such as tenfold size, a year later or a changed dependency. Keep findings tied to the goal, cost or risk.
   - Give each weakness evidence, a remedy and seriousness: stops it, costs or minor. Group common causes and put the most serious first.
   - Use the `understand-research` guide or a permitted test for factual doubts. This guide owns independent objections under the `act-verify` guide's rule 2; use the `coordinate-delegate` guide for the required worker.
   - When the `respond-ask` guide calls, apply rule 6's stakes levels. Reuse a challenge only while the idea, goal, constraints and evidence stay unchanged.
3. **Return an honest verdict under rules 4 and 5.** Invented objections waste the owner's effort; softened ones hide a reason to change course.
   - Give holds, holds with changes, or does not hold, with reasons. Say plainly when nothing meaningful was found.
   - Return weaknesses and how each was met, fixed or accepted as a risk. For your own quietly analyzed idea, show the verdict and serious weaknesses with reasons. Change nothing here.
4. **Check the challenge and “Gives back” before returning it.** Always do this last. A verdict without support or remedies cannot guide the caller's decision.
   - Check the fair idea, frame, assumption labels, evidence, seriousness and remedies. Check reused results are current and required independence follows the `act-verify` guide's rule 2.
   - Fix weak or unsupported findings before returning under the `understand` guide's rules. Keep lasting records and revisions with their owner.
