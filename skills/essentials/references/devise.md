# devise

## Purpose

Chooses how to do the work, in thinking, before it starts, when the way is not obvious. Uses design or planning where the work needs them.

## When to use

Use before starting work that has more than one plausible way, several parts or an open shape. For example, planning a trip with flights, lodging and a budget has several parts. Several parts means more than one step or part, by the `act` guide's rule 13 test of multipart work.

## When not

Work with one obvious way and one part: the main skill's one-line approach in thinking is enough. When neither design nor planning fits, use your judgment under the `act` guide.

## Gives back

The approach stays in thinking. Return any used child’s result to the active task.

## Instructions

Work needs an approach before it starts: which way to go, which guides shape each part, and how the result will be checked. Going the wrong way costs rework, and the first idea is not always the best one. **Think the approach through before you start, and use design or planning where the work needs them.**

Design and plan may call each other for a different part of the work. A guide called this way returns its result to its caller. It does not start the caller again.

1. **Settle the approach in your thinking before you start.** A way chosen before you see what the work needs is often the wrong one.
   - Start from the intent, the goal and what a good result looks like.
   - Look at what the work touches before you choose a way, such as the files, the people or the systems it reaches.
   - When the way is open, consider more than one. Judge them by the goal, the risk and the cost, and know why you chose.
   - For each part, decide which guide shapes it, which skills and tools do it, its order and its checks. Naming the checks now lets you keep the evidence they will need.
   - One line is enough for small work, such as "rightsize, then answer".
2. **Use the `devise-design` guide when what will be made is still open.** Steps for a shape nobody has decided build the wrong thing.
   - The shape is open when its parts, how they connect or how it behaves are not decided yet, such as a new release process or a handbook's structure.
   - A request to design and build a whole starts with design, then plans the build.
3. **Use the `devise-plan` guide for work with several parts, or for a plan from an earlier turn that is still open.** A plan keeps the steps and their checks together, and keeps them current as the work and the user's input change.
   - A plan may need a design for one part. Return to the plan afterward.
4. **Check the approach before acting or returning a result.** Always do this last. An approach that misses a part of the request, or adds one, sends the work the wrong way from its start.
   - Every requested part has a method and a check.
   - Nothing unasked was added.
   - Follow the final step of each child guide you used before returning its result.
