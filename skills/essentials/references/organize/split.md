# organize-split

Divides one written or planned whole into parts, or sorts all loose items into groups.

```text
A → A1 A2 A3
```

## Advice

- Make each part understandable on its own. Say what it is without needing a sibling to make sense.
- Size a part for how it is read. Keep it short when readers load it whole or on every turn. Add headings or a map when readers search it.

## Instructions

1. **Frame the whole and what its parts are for.** Choose the dividing principle to serve the whole's purpose.
2. **List everything the whole contains or must cover.** Do this before grouping. For a future whole, list its requirements.
3. **Choose one dividing principle.** Follow the parent's shared choice rule.
4. **Write each part's boundary in one sentence.**
5. **Map every item to exactly one part.** Name any item that fits no part or fits more than one part.
6. **Resolve missing or double homes.** Sharpen overlapping boundaries. Add a part for a required item with no home. Exclusions need user approval and remain preserved outside the changed scope. Otherwise return the unresolved placement in the proposal. Do not silently drop an item.
7. **Use the `organize` skill's bounded check for overlaps and gaps.** Return to this operation after the check. Do not restart the `organize` skill's step for choosing an operation. Apply the arrangement only under the parent's approved map.

## The last instruction

Return parts, boundaries, each item's place and any exceptions to the parent. A proposal or check may report unresolved placements. A completed change has one home for each required item and preserves approved exclusions. Follow the `organize` skill's inventory check and approval checkpoints. Check that parts can be read on their own and fit how they are read.
